Privacy Policy

Your priorities are personal. Here is what BenchPoll stores, what other people can see, and what control you have.

Effective: October 30, 2026 · Wording updated: November 12, 2026

  • Browsing and trying a benchmark mix do not require an account.
  • Signed-in weights in public categories contribute to aggregate Public Weights. Private categories do not feed Public Weights.
  • Private categories are visible to you and authorized operators.
  • Private categories are not used for research, even if you previously opted in.
  • Privacy requests do not require sign-in. Account deactivation is not complete erasure.

This policy covers benchpoll.com and its account, contribution, ranking, discussion and review features. The service is currently free, with no payment feature. Reading or acknowledging this policy is not, by itself, consent to every processing activity. Agreeing to the Terms of Use is separate from optional privacy choices.

1. Operator and privacy contact

The BenchPoll operator ("we" or "us") works from mainland China and decides how BenchPoll uses personal information. The operator's legal name and postal address have not been published.

For questions or access, correction, erasure or objection requests, use the privacy request form. You can submit a reply email address and message without signing in, including if you cannot access your GitHub account or do not want to reactivate it. Requests stay private. A human reviews the request and verifies identity before disclosure or deletion.

For privacy requests, email privacy@benchpoll.com. For general questions, email support@benchpoll.com. You can use email if the form is unavailable; do not put passwords, unnecessary identity documents or private request details in public discussions.

2. Information we collect

Browsing and technical information

Our hosting and web-server infrastructure can process your IP address, request time, requested URL, response status, browser or user-agent information and technical errors. This is used to deliver and secure the service. A page request can disclose information in its URL, so do not place secrets or sensitive personal information in links.

GitHub sign-in

GitHub supplies your numeric account ID, account name, avatar URL, follower count and account-creation information. The user:email permission obtains a verified email address, which may not be public on GitHub. We store the ID, name, avatar URL, follower count, verified address and verification timestamp. Account history supports abuse checks; it is not proof of your age. The OAuth token is used for the login exchange, not stored in our account database as a reusable credential. We do not receive your GitHub password.

You can replace the default public name and avatar in your profile. We store your chosen name and a resized copy of the uploaded image. These appear with your public discussions, including earlier posts, and with your private topics where shown. Resetting them restores the GitHub defaults. Do not upload an image or name you do not want others to see.

The current GitHub sign-in flow requires a verified email address. Without the required account information, sign-in cannot complete, but you can still browse and submit a privacy request. The address supports account-linked review notifications; it is not added to a marketing list.

We record the Terms version you accept and when, the Privacy Policy version acknowledged, document hashes and your 16+ and legal eligibility confirmation. We may also retain the history of earlier separate research choices as evidence. Those old choices no longer enable private-category research. Agreement and eligibility are confirmed by the sign-in continuation button beside the notice, or by explicit checkboxes in account settings. These records distinguish account agreement and privacy-notice acknowledgment from consent to optional processing.

We retain the GitHub username verified at sign-in. Linking your public avatar and name to your GitHub profile is off by default. If you enable it in your account, the link is public; turning it off removes the link from new public responses. It cannot remove copies already made by others.

Weights, categories and comparison settings

We process benchmark and test-version selections, their weights, fallback combinations, selected public categories and contexts, private-category names and settings, saved comparison configurations and their names, and creation or update times. Signed-in settings are associated with your GitHub account ID. Guest mixes are held in the current browser tab and are sent to the server when needed to calculate ranking previews, but are not stored as signed-in personal pies or counted in Public Weights.

Topics can be created and published immediately without review. Public topics show their name, summary, scenes and creator profile; official topics are labeled Official. Private topics remain visible only to you and authorized operators. Public topic popularity uses total and recent favorites, not your browsing history.

Contributions, messages and review records

We store submitted benchmark and model information, scores, fields, versions, sources, object notes, change requests, feedback, reports and discussion messages, with relevant account IDs, times, status and replies. Older benchmark tag records may remain in historical storage, but the current contribution forms and public benchmark views do not provide a tag feature. Account-linked votes prevent repeat voting; public views show totals, not a voter list. Privacy cases contain a reference, an optional account link, reply email, request type, message, status, resolution, handling operator, timestamps and an internal due date. Only senior operators may list or update those cases.

We store topic names, summaries, scenes, creator accounts, visibility choices and creation or update times to provide topic creation and publication. Favorites store the account, topic and activity times. Public topic cards show favorite totals; your profile privacy settings control whether other people can see your collection of favorites.

Administrative audit records can include reviewer IDs, submission contents, before-and-after objects, operation type, executed SQL, results or errors, times and decisions. Email queues can contain recipient addresses, notification contents and delivery history. Do not include passwords, API keys, payment details or unrelated personal data in submissions.

Information comes from you, your browser, GitHub, people submitting or reporting content, and the public benchmark or model sources cited in the catalogue. A cited page or another person's report may incidentally identify you; public availability does not remove your privacy rights. You may ask about the source or request a correction through the private request form.

3. Why we use information

Where EU or UK data-protection law applies, we use the following legal grounds for each purpose. Requirements in other jurisdictions are addressed separately below.

Requested account and comparison features
Use GitHub identity and session data to sign you in; save weights, categories and profiles; calculate rankings, fallbacks and public-category aggregates. The basis is performing the service agreement for features you request. Supplying optional text or a saved profile is not required to browse.
Contributions, discussions and review notices
Process submitted content, account links and review decisions to provide the participation features you request. Use your verified email for related service notifications through Resend when delivery is enabled, on the same service-agreement basis. Publication is limited to the public fields described below; private report details are not a public contribution.
Security, integrity and troubleshooting
Use technical records, account history, reports and proportionate audit evidence to prevent manipulation, protect private settings, investigate abuse and fix errors. Where permitted, this relies on legitimate interests in a reliable, safe service, balanced against your rights, and on a specific legal obligation where one applies. An audit label does not justify unlimited collection or retention.
Rights requests and legal responsibilities
Use contact details, necessary verification and case records to answer privacy requests and meet applicable legal obligations. Limited records for a real legal claim may be kept under a permitted legitimate interest in establishing or defending that claim, subject to applicable law.

Under China's Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), contractual necessity and specified legal duties can support necessary uses, but legitimate interests are not a general basis. Other processing needs consent or another basis the applicable law actually permits. Any required separate permission for publication or a transfer must be obtained before that activity; accepting the Terms or this notice does not provide it. The unresolved jurisdiction and transfer assessment is explained in section 7.

Rankings are calculations about AI models, not automated decisions about a user's employment, credit, health, legal status or access to essential services.

4. What other people can see

Public benchmark data
Approved benchmark, model, version and score data, source URLs and object notes can be published on the site or returned by its public APIs. Keep personal or confidential information out of fields intended for publication.
Discussions
Your posted message, context, benchmark mentions, timestamps, replies and account name can be visible to others. Public content can be copied, indexed or retained by third parties outside our control. Public posting is a choice; do not put private information in a message intended for everyone.
Personal weights in public categories
The service publishes aggregated weights and counts, not an account-by-account list of pies. An aggregate from a small number of pies can still reveal or suggest an individual's choices.
Private settings and review-only information
Private categories, saved filters, account email addresses, feedback, abuse reports and internal audit records are not made public by those features. They remain accessible to authorized operators, reviewers or service providers as needed for the purposes described here.

Submitting a private feedback or report does not authorize publishing its identifying content. If resolving a submission would require publishing personal information that is not already intentionally public, we must establish an appropriate legal basis and obtain any legally required permission first.

5. Private categories and public topics

A private category is private from other users, not from BenchPoll's authorized operator. Its name, selected benchmarks, weights, fallback rules and related configuration are stored with your account and can be accessed for operating, supporting and securing the service.

We do not use private-category names or configurations for research, including research to develop public topics, even if you previously opted in. There is no research opt-in. You can choose to publish a private topic. Publishing makes that topic and its scenes public; keeping a topic private does not publish it.

Ending this research does not undo research already completed or automatically erase past choices, inputs or results. Identifiable historical inputs or results must still be reviewed for deletion when they no longer have a lawful retention purpose. You can ask about past use without signing in through the request form.

Private categories have Personal Weights only and do not feed Public Weights. Any future use of private-category inputs in a public category's weights requires fresh, specific notice of the data and calculation, a re-identification assessment and fresh consent wherever required before that use starts. A past research choice is not retroactive or blanket authorization and does not authorize public disclosure. Removing an account ID does not anonymize a distinctive category name or a tiny group.

When category maintenance removes or transfers a category, it must preserve your existing personal mix privately, including weights and fallback settings. Maintenance must not silently overwrite another private mix or generate a new public vote; it is not unrestricted permission to delete your settings. You may also use the request form to object to non-essential category research or ask about past use. Creating a category does not waive statutory rights.

6. Cookies and device storage

We use the first-party sid session cookie to keep you signed in, protect GitHub login and provide CSRF protection for privacy forms, including requests made without signing in. It is HTTP-only, uses SameSite protections and is marked secure on HTTPS connections. The current configured session lifetime is seven days, subject to logout and session expiry. Server-side session records support logout on one or all devices. Temporary security and rate checks protect these forms; privacy-request case records do not contain your raw IP address.

Feature storage is used to support the service. sessionStorage holds guest mixes, selected workspace preferences and form-handoff state for a tab; localStorage holds requested guest comparison profiles, account-scoped recent-topic history and temporary form-prefill handoffs between tabs. Recent history holds up to 80 topic/context entries without topic names or avatars; it is not sent as a history log or used to calculate heat. A form-prefill token expires after 30 minutes; later handoff activity cleans up the entry, not an always-running timer. Other local preferences can remain until you remove them or clear site data. These records support the feature you use, not advertising, marketing or independent analytics.

You can clear BenchPoll's cookies and site data in your browser. This signs you out and can discard unsaved local drafts; it does not delete server-side account data. Blocking essential storage can prevent login or draft handoff from working.

The enforced Content Security Policy excludes the external Cloudflare Web Analytics script origin reviewed for this release. Cloudflare proxying, security processing and network logs continue; same-origin scripts and HTTPS images remain permitted. We do not use advertising or marketing tracking. Fonts and interface assets are served locally; GitHub avatars and external links can still contact third parties. New nonessential tracking requires further notice and any required prior consent. See the Cookie Policy for storage purposes, lifetimes and choices.

7. Providers, disclosures and international transfers

Authorized administrators and reviewers access information needed for their work. Amazon Web Services Lightsail hosts the production application and database in Ohio, United States. Operator administration takes place from mainland China; authorized administration and backup handling can process information outside the hosting region. Your information may therefore be processed outside your own country.

GitHub provides authentication, verified email information and remote avatars. Cloudflare provides DNS, proxying and network protection, and can process IP addresses, request metadata and security logs across its network. Blocking analytics scripts does not remove that infrastructure processing.

Resend delivers review notifications when delivery is enabled. It processes the recipient email address, sender address, subject, notification contents and delivery metadata, including delivery errors and bounces. These messages identify a reviewed contribution and its outcome, not your private-category names, saved weights or unrelated account data. We do not use them for marketing or add tracking pixels to their templates. Notices may wait in a queue or fail during delivery. Review-notification sending and a monitored privacy inbox are separate services.

We may disclose information when required by law or a valid legal process, to protect users and the service, or to investigate unlawful activity, using a proportionate scope. If the operator or service changes ownership, we will notify users of the recipient and any material change of purpose before personal information is transferred as required by law.

We do not sell personal information or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. We do not send private-category data to a public AI-model endpoint as a built-in service feature. A future processor or purpose change requires an appropriate arrangement, further notice and any required consent.

The applicable-law assessment, provider data-processing agreements and international-transfer safeguards have not yet been completed or verified. The operator must resolve the applicable requirements, obtain any required separate consent and restrict or pause processing that lacks a required legal basis or safeguard. Use the privacy request form to ask about recipients, locations or safeguards.

Third-party sites operate under their own policies: GitHub, AWS, Cloudflare and Resend. Those links do not replace BenchPoll's responsibility for its own processing.

8. Retention, deactivation and deletion

Retention depends on active account use, an unresolved request or review, the need for an approved non-personal public record, a specific security incident or legal claim, and any mandatory retention period. A complete per-record schedule and automated full-erasure process are not yet in place; some cleanup requires an authorized human.

  • Account settings: stored while you use the account. The current deactivation action marks the account inactive, ends its sessions and removes saved comparison profiles. It does not completely erase identity records, email, weights, private categories, contributions or audit history. Signing in with GitHub again reactivates the account. Revoking the GitHub authorization prevents further authorized access through that grant, but does not itself erase information already stored by BenchPoll.
  • Discussion deletion: deleting your own message removes its visible body, linked mentions and votes. A deleted thread placeholder can remain to preserve replies. Information preserved in reports, review evidence or audit records may remain for a justified review or security purpose.
  • Public data: approved benchmark facts, scores and sources can remain useful after a submitter deactivates an account. We consider requests to remove attribution or personal information separately from non-personal benchmark facts. Copies already obtained by third parties may be outside our control.
  • Choices and private cases: version and choice history can be retained as necessary evidence of an agreement, withdrawal or response. A request's internal due date is a handling target, not a deletion timer. Request messages and verification evidence must not be retained longer than needed for handling, applicable duties or a justified dispute.
  • Sessions, logs and backups: sessions expire or are removed on logout. Technical logs, notification history and audit records are retained only for applicable operational, security or legal needs. Backup copies can outlive a change in the live database; an erasure response will explain any restricted backup retention and when it will be removed or rendered unusable. A restored backup must not be used to reintroduce data that should remain erased.

For a full erasure request, use the contact procedure below rather than assuming the account-deactivation button removes everything. We will explain what is deleted, any lawful exception, and the retention criteria that apply; missing automation or backup existence is not a blanket reason to refuse a valid request or retain data indefinitely. Where required, we must also take reasonable steps concerning recipients of corrected or erased information.

9. Security and access

We use HTTPS, authenticated account access, owner checks for private categories and settings, administrative permissions and review audit records. We restrict operational access and take reasonable precautions when handling database copies and requests. The service is not end-to-end encrypted; authorized operators can access stored records for the purposes described above.

Protect your GitHub account and device, do not share login cookies, and report suspected exposure through a private channel. Where a security incident requires notification to affected users or authorities, we will provide it as required by applicable law.

10. Your choices and privacy rights

You can browse without signing in, change your personal weights and saved filters, delete your own discussion messages, clear browser site data, log out on your devices, deactivate your account or revoke GitHub access. Public-category weights can be cleared; that is different from erasing all account records.

Depending on applicable law, you may request access to personal information, a copy or portability, correction, erasure, restriction, an explanation of processing, objection to a permitted legitimate-interest use, or withdrawal of consent. Withdrawal does not invalidate processing that was lawful before withdrawal. You can exercise rights without losing them for making a request; a feature may stop working only where its necessary processing can no longer lawfully continue. There is no sale of personal information or behavioral-advertising sharing for you to opt out of in the current service.

Use the privacy request form without signing in. Supply a reply email and describe the account or record and the action requested. A human will verify ownership and handle appropriate access, correction or deletion. Requests, replies and erasure are handled manually. Do not send your GitHub password or unnecessary identity documents. Ordinary requests are free; any legally permitted exception will be explained.

We will respond within the time required by applicable law, explain any permitted extension, lawful refusal or limited retention, and provide a way to challenge the response. Where the EU GDPR applies, this is normally within one month of receipt, subject to its specific extension and verification rules. Other laws can require a different or earlier response. An internal target does not replace the applicable deadline.

Keep the reference shown after submission and use it to follow up or challenge handling. You may complain directly to the competent data-protection authority, such as an EU supervisory authority, the UK Information Commissioner's Office or the responsible Chinese authority where applicable, or use other available legal remedies. You do not have to complain publicly or contact us first to preserve those rights. If the form fails, email privacy@benchpoll.com.

11. Children and sensitive information

Accounts are limited to people aged 16 or older who also meet the legal eligibility and any parent or guardian authorization requirements that apply to them. We do not ask for birth dates. If we learn that an under-16 account or other child's information was collected without a lawful basis, we will restrict processing and take appropriate removal steps.

The service does not need health records, financial-account details, government IDs, biometrics, precise location or other sensitive personal information to create a benchmark mix. Do not submit such information, or third parties' private information, in category names, notes, source URLs or discussions. If it is supplied inadvertently, contact us for removal rather than repeating it publicly.

12. Changes to this policy

We publish the effective date at the top. We will explain material changes to data categories, purposes, public visibility, providers or privacy choices through an appropriate service notice or contact method before the new processing starts, with any urgent legally required notice handled as the law permits. If a change requires fresh or separate consent, merely updating this page or your continuing to browse is not sufficient. New purposes are not applied retroactively on the strength of an old acknowledgment.

For service-use rules, read the Terms of Use and Community Guidelines. For storage details, read the Cookie Policy; for ranking calculations, read How it works. None overrides privacy rights that cannot legally be waived.
