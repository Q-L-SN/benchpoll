How BenchPoll works

BenchPoll combines benchmark results into model rankings. You choose the weights, or use the community’s weights.

Set your weights

Add the benchmarks you want to use. Select a block in the chart and scroll over the chart, or drag up and down on a phone, to change its weight. The weights add up to 100%.

Starting and saving your weights

Add benchmarks or topic references from Evaluation items to Personal Weights. Index composition totals 100% when nonempty. With two or more items, select a block and adjust its weight; other shares rebalance.

Try it without signing in. Sign in to save your weights. Unsaved changes are marked on the page.

Use community weights

Public Weights combine people’s saved configurations for the current topic and scene. Saving your own weights contributes to this mix.

How public weights come together

Public Weights combine saved personal weight configurations in this topic, shaped by recent activity. Each person contributes one configuration, updated with their edits.

Older configurations remain eligible to fill sparse samples up to 10 total.

Each topic or scene has its own Public Weights. Another topic contributes only through an explicit reference. Private topics and their weights never contribute to Public Weights.

How scores and coverage are calculated

Both modes use the same qualifying measurements, converted using each test's score range and direction, then combined using your weights. Fallback is resolved first.

  • Estimated score: combines measured results with shared-test estimates. Isolated extreme results have less influence on estimates; incomplete measured coverage reduces the whole total.
  • Score range: missing scores count as 0 at the lower end and 100 at the upper end; measured scores stay fixed. Models rank by the lower end.

Coverage is the share of weight with measured results, including successful fallbacks. Estimates do not increase coverage. Unresolved gaps are left out of Estimated score by default. Without a real result on a weighted test, a model cannot rank.

Coverage factor: f(c)=5c/(1+4c), with measured coverage c. Nonnegative totals use f(c), negatives 2-f(c); full coverage is unchanged.

Test algorithm applies to this tab. Missing-only methods keep measured contributions fixed, bound predictions to 0–100, and use a fixed midpoint of 50 for unresolved gaps.

Unadjusted keeps that missing contribution; Rational multiplies it by f(c); Linear by c; Square root by √c. Midpoint blends it toward 50 using c and 1−c, so scores can rise or fall. All five stay within Score range; full coverage is unchanged.

Turn on effort or harness to rank settings separately; otherwise use each model's best qualifying score per test. Missing settings stay in an unknown group.

More details

Can I combine topics in one index?

You can add another topic with saved weights, choosing a specific scene if it has scenes. A topic or scene cannot directly reference itself. References follow live weights and stay linked to the scene you selected.

Public indices always follow public target weights. Personal indices prefer your saved target weights and otherwise follow public weights. Private topics may reference your other private topics; public-topic configurations cannot reference private topics.

Weights multiply along reference paths and combine for each benchmark version. Different fallback choices retain their shares. Scores and coverage correction are calculated after expansion; child scores are never averaged.

References combine capabilities. To evaluate a specific combination, such as real-time vector generation, use benchmarks that test those requirements together.

Topics can reference one another in a loop. We calculate the benchmark shares reached after following every repeated path. A loop with no path to benchmarks adds no measured score or coverage; its share stays missing.

Empty or unavailable targets retain their missing weight and show a notice.

How do versions and fallback work?

Added benchmarks follow the community. Expand Contribute your expertise below the weight chart to select a version, then choose a saved fallback order or edit your own. Both are optional.

Explicit choices updated within 14 days shape version and fallback proportions per topic/scene, weighted by benchmark allocation. Following adds no vote.

Unselected versions follow community proportions; without votes, use the primary version. Changing versions clears fallback. If nobody has chosen, the index works normally without backups.

Try the chosen version first, then backups in order. The first available score counts, including zero and negative scores. No fallback uses the chosen version alone, with estimation still available; deselect your configuration to follow the community again.

Backup versions are selected explicitly. New versions never join an existing order automatically.

How can I publish a topic?

Create a private topic, then choose Make public in its editor when ready. Creation and publication are immediate and do not require review. You can have up to three public topics at a time.

Private topics and scenes are visible only to you. You can make a public topic private again; make it private before deleting it.

Open your profile and choose Settings to edit your details and privacy choices. Showing a GitHub link is optional.
